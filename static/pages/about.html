About

What MarketSignal is

A research instrument, built in the open, that tries to earn trust the slow way — by recording every prediction and being honest about the score.

The idea

Most market tools tell you what to think and never tell you how often they were right. MarketSignal does the opposite: it makes a specific, dated call for each market it follows, then checks it. The value isn't any single forecast — it's the accumulating, auditable record.

What it does each day

  • Scans a fixed universe of ~40 liquid markets — large-cap stocks across sectors, the main index ETFs, and the two largest cryptocurrencies.
  • Logs a forecast per market and horizon (10-day and 30-day): direction, expected percentage move, a confidence band, and a due date.
  • Grades every forecast that has come due against the real closing price, and rolls the result into the track record.

How the forecast is made

A learned model — currently a regularised linear regression on the forward return — is fit on the full history of graded outcomes and re-validated walk-forward over five years. It reads price structure and momentum, not tips. Live fundamentals and news then add a small, capped adjustment on top, and both the raw and adjusted calls are stored so the overlay can be judged on its own.

Discipline

New model ideas are held to a single test: do they beat the current live model on out-of-sample strategy P&L? If not, they don't ship. So far a gradient-boosted-tree model and a macro-regime feature set have both been built, measured, and set aside for failing that test — which is the point.

Data

Prices and fundamentals from Yahoo Finance; headlines from Google News. No paid feeds, no alternative data yet.

Important

MarketSignal is for research and education. It is not investment advice, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security, and not a solicitation. Model output can be wrong and frequently is. Past performance — backtested or live — does not predict future results. Markets can and do lose money. Do your own research and consider a licensed advisor before investing.
